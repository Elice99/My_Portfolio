Sunrise Coffee: how the analysis was built

A small reproducible Python pipeline over five CSV tables. It writes one summary JSON and six report charts. This page covers the grain, the metric definitions, the validation checks, and the problems I found in the supplied workbook.

The solution

I left the supplied workbook untouched. Its cached dashboard KPIs disagree with the raw data, so the case study uses figures recomputed from the five CSV tables by scripts/analyse.py.

The pipeline has two steps. analyse.py joins the dimensions to the facts and writes every figure to outputs/summary.json. make_charts.py reads that JSON plus the raw CSVs and writes six PNG figures. There is no randomness and no manual spreadsheet work.

The source data and its grain

TableRowsGrain or role
dim_store.csv60One row per store, with cost structure and loyalty adoption
dim_product.csv40One row per product, with price, cost and margin
dim_date.csv730One row per calendar day, 1 Jan 2023 to 30 Dec 2024
fact_daily_sales.csv10,000Store-day sales with top product and category revenue split
fact_store_monthly_profitability.csv1,440Store-month P&L, 60 stores times 24 months

Join keys

store_id, date_id and product_id are unique in the dimensions and fully populated in both facts. There are zero orphan keys.

Daily coverage

10,000 recorded store-days against 43,800 possible store-days. I never describe a daily cut as an estate-wide census.

Period

The date table ends on 30 December 2024. The brief says 31 December 2024, but I follow the data.

Metric definitions

I kept profitability, loyalty and revenue mix as separate lenses instead of forcing them into one headline.

Estate KPIs

scripts/analyse.py

Revenue, profit and margin come from the 1,440 store-month P&L rows. Cost shares use the same denominator.

Estate KPIsPYTHON
rev = float(m.total_monthly_revenue_gbp.sum())
profit = float(m.net_profit_gbp.sum())
return {
    "revenue_gbp": rev,
    "net_profit_gbp": profit,
    "net_profit_margin_pct": profit / rev * 100,
    "cogs_gbp": float(m.total_monthly_cogs_gbp.sum()),
    "rent_gbp": float(m.monthly_rent_gbp.sum()),
    "staffing_gbp": float(m.monthly_staffing_cost_gbp.sum()),
    "loyalty_redemption_gbp": float(m.loyalty_redemption_cost_gbp.sum()),
}

Review note. The 18.8 percent margin is profit divided by revenue, not an average of store margin percentages.

Regional cut

scripts/analyse.py

Region is joined from dim_store. Margin uses summed revenue and summed profit so larger stores carry their proper weight.

Regional cutPYTHON
g = m.groupby("region").agg(
    revenue=("total_monthly_revenue_gbp", "sum"),
    profit=("net_profit_gbp", "sum"),
)
g["margin_pct"] = g.profit / g.revenue * 100
loss = m.profitability_status.isin(["Loss-Making", "Structural-Loss"])
g["loss_month_share_pct"] = loss.groupby(m["region"]).mean() * 100

Review note. A simple mean of net_profit_margin_pct would give every store-month equal weight and would overstate small stores.

Loyalty correlations

scripts/analyse.py

Store-level means are used for adoption and member revenue share. Transaction value is recomputed from revenue and transactions because the supplied ATV column is rounded.

Loyalty correlationsPYTHON
adopt = m.groupby("store_id").loyalty_adoption_rate.mean()  # from dim_store
member = m.groupby("store_id").loyalty_member_revenue_pct.mean()
atv = d.groupby("store_id").apply(
    lambda x: x.total_revenue_gbp.sum() / x.total_transactions.sum(),
    include_groups=False,
)
r_member = adopt.corr(member)   # 0.998
r_atv = adopt.corr(atv)         # -0.063

Review note. The 0.998 result is reported as a near-mechanical relationship. It is not presented as proof that loyalty causes higher member revenue.

Validation and data quality

I recomputed the internal identities instead of trusting the dictionary. All four core checks pass.

CheckResult
Four category columns sum to daily revenue100 percent of rows
Net profit equals revenue less COGS, rent, staffing and redemption100 percent of rows
Published ATV matches revenue divided by transactions100 percent within rounding, maximum gap 0.12 percent
Nulls in loyalty share, footfall and seating capacity0
Orphan keys in both fact tables0

Three dictionary claims are wrong for this extract. dim_date has 11 columns and no month_id. The daily fact has an extra column, avg_dwell_time_minutes. Product gross margin runs from 66.0 to 90.4 percent, not the 36 to 80 percent stated in the dictionary.

What I found in the supplied workbook

The workbook is a useful starting point for layout, but its cached KPI layer is not safe to publish. I kept it as a reference file and rebuilt the numbers from the CSVs.

IssueWhat I sawWhat I did
Conflicting store counts59 Profitable plus 41 Loss-Making from a 60-store estate, and status categories summing to 221Ignored those KPI cells and recomputed status from the raw monthly file
KPI scale2024-only revenue of £44.70M and profit of £8.41M shown as headline totalsReported the full 2023 and 2024 totals of £89.69M and £16.88M with the period stated
Expansion countOne KPI says 20 candidate stores while the visible pivot totals 1Left the field out of the case study until the definition is fixed
Chart labelling27 of 28 charts have no title, and Product, Loyalty and Store tabs have no cell valuesGenerated six titled report charts with explicit captions
Field labelsweek_num holds day of week, IDs are text, and several labels have typosUsed the CSV column names and documented the mismatch
Duplicate blockThe loyalty-per-store range appears twiceCharts use the analysis output, not the duplicated range

Reproduce it

Full analysisBASH
python scripts/analyse.py
python scripts/make_charts.py

# Outputs:
# outputs/summary.json
# outputs/charts/01_region_margin.png ... 06_store_moves.png

No random steps, no network calls and no manual workbook edits. Re-running reproduces the same summary and charts.

Dependencies

Python 3.10+, pandas, numpy and matplotlib.

Source safety

The five CSVs and the original workbook are read-only inputs.

Determinism

No seed is needed because no step is random.

What I would change with better data

Full daily census

Complete store-day rows would let me answer the weekday and holiday questions without sample caveats.

Member transaction counts

Member and non-member transactions would turn contribution into a real incrementality test.

Correct format labels

A properly populated Retail-Park format would allow the comparison the brief asks for.

Clean KPI layer

One agreed definition per card, with titles on every chart, would make the workbook safe for leadership.

The main judgement call was to treat the workbook as a source artefact rather than a source of truth. It would have been easier to quote its KPI cards. It would also have meant publishing contradictory store counts.
